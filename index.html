DeSHAPE-α: Deconvolution of quantile Structures of Heterogeneity, Asymmetry, and Pattern to advance microbial α-diversity analysis

Z. Zhu, Y. Zhang, S. Lin, and L. Zhang

Bioinformatics Advances, vbag329 (2 Nov 2026). Accepted manuscript.

Published DOI: 10.1093/bioadv/vbag329
Abstract summary

DeSHAPE extends α-diversity comparisons beyond mean or median differences by testing distinct center, dispersion, and asymmetry components while adjusting for covariates. Mode-matched simulations show approximately calibrated type I error and strong power for dispersion effects, with more gradual sensitivity to asymmetry. Applications to pancreatic cancer, cardiovascular disease, and seasonal diet microbiome datasets reveal distributional differences that conventional α-diversity tests do not isolate.

Koinetic: A Graph-based Distance as a New Ecological Metric for Microbial Community Differential Analysis

Z. Zhu, Y. Zhang, R. Liu, W. Li, Z. Huang, X. Chen, Y. Duan, and L. Zhang*

Briefings in Bioinformatics (2026).

Published DOI: 10.1093/bib/bbag632 Publisher metadata pending
Abstract

Phylogeny-aware beta diversity measures are essential in microbiome research, yet widely used metrics such as UniFrac and its variants rely on independent branch length contributions and do not satisfy the Euclidean property, leading to distortions in ordination and kernel-based tests. These limitations underscore the need for a distance that is both phylogenetically coherent and geometrically valid. We introduce the Koinos Kinetic Distance (Koinetic), a phylogeny-aware dissimilarity that views microbial communities as interconnected assemblies rather than isolated taxa. “Koinos” emphasizes collective evaluation, while “kinetic” reflects how differences propagate dynamically across ecological networks and phylogenetic lineages. Koinetic leverages a graph Laplacian–based kernel to weight community differences by shared phylogeny, capturing ecological connectivity and clade relatedness. This design ensures Euclidean validity and enables seamless integration with kernel-based methods as well as geometry-preserving ordination. We demonstrate the effectiveness of Koinetic through a combination of controlled simulation studies and applications to eight microbiome datasets spanning pancreatic cancer, IBD, and metabolic disease–associated oral microbiomes. Compared to classical and phylogenetic metrics, including weighted, unweighted, and generalized UniFrac, Koinetic achieves superior preservation of geometry under dimensionality reduction and improved clustering. It bridges phylogeny-aware metrics with kernel-compatible representations, thereby providing a general foundation for phylogeny-aware analysis.

Rebuilding microbiome diversity theory on the closed simplex

Y. Zhang and Z. Zhu

bioRxiv (3 Sep 2026). Submitted to npj Biofilms and Microbiomes.

Preprint bioRxiv · Version 1 HRIC project website
Abstract summary & figure

Microbiome studies often report within-sample diversity and between-sample distances without clearly defining how local communities relate to a larger regional ensemble. This work develops a common framework for making those relationships explicit. Hellinger–Riemann intrinsic coordinates (HRIC) represent compositional data on the closed simplex while preserving zero-valued taxa. Within this representation, local and regional diversity, additive between-community diversity, individual taxon contributions, distances between samples, and variation explained by statistical models can be analyzed together. Simulations connect HRIC-based dispersion to the between-condition component of PERMANOVA. Applications to Arctic and North Atlantic microbial communities reveal comparable relationships between relative local diversity and vertical environmental gradients. In an autologous fecal microbiota transplantation trial, recipients recovered toward their own baseline compositions earlier, while alpha-diversity differences were less clear. Together, the results connect diversity partitioning with compositional analysis across environmental, host-associated, and longitudinal microbiome studies.

Mathematical Foundations of Beta Diversity: Why Common Metrics Fail in Microbiome Analysis

Z. Zhu, Y. Zhang, W. Li, M. Greenacre, S. Saha, Y. Shi, and L. Zhang

Journal of Statistical Theory and Applications, vol. 25, article 9 (2026).

Published DOI: 10.1007/s44199-025-00154-7
Abstract summary & figure

Beta-diversity analysis is central to comparing microbiome communities, yet widely used dissimilarity measures do not always satisfy the metric, Euclidean, or conditionally negative definite properties required by common downstream methods. We systematically evaluate popular measures, organize them into four mathematical classes, and show how property violations can distort PCoA, PERMANOVA, and kernel-based inference. We also introduce diagnostic tools and correction strategies that restore valid geometry while preserving ordination structure. Analyses across real microbiome datasets translate the theory into practical guidance, supported by an R package, interactive Shiny application, and reproducible tutorials for selecting and refining beta-diversity metrics.

Review and Revamp of Compositional Data Transformation: A New Framework Combining Proportion Conversion and Contrast Transformation

Y. Zhang, J. Schluter, L. Zhang, X. Cao, R. R. Jenq, H. Feng, J. Haines, and L. Zhang

Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal, vol. 23, pp. 4088–4107 (2024).

Published DOI: 10.1016/j.csbj.2024.11.003
Abstract summary & figure

Microbiome abundance data combine unequal sequencing depth, compositional constraints, and frequent zeros, which can make standard scaling and transformation methods yield inconsistent conclusions. This work reviews the connections and distinctions among existing approaches and introduces a unified framework that pairs proportion conversion with contrast transformation. The framework recovers familiar methods such as ALR and CLR while enabling new transformations, including Centered Arcsine Contrast and Additive Arcsine Contrast. Simulation and data analyses indicate that the arcsine-based methods are particularly useful under heavy zero inflation, whereas log-ratio methods perform better when zeros are less prevalent, offering practical guidance for robust microbiome analysis.
